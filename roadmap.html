Roadmap

Building toward
perfect correction.

A live view of where DOA has been, where it stands today, and what comes next. Updated as things change.

Last updated: April 2026

Current correction quality

~80%

DOA uses your prescription numbers as they are — the same values written for physical lenses. This gets you most of the way there. The remaining gap exists because your prescription was measured for a specific distance and set of conditions that may not match your screen setup exactly. Two people with the same prescription can experience different results depending on their screen, their distance from it, and how their eyes individually respond. Closing that gap — making the correction fit you, not just your prescription on paper — is what the next stages of development are about.

Where we've been
November 2024 The idea
Done

The concept: a screen can pre-adjust its image so that by the time the light reaches your eye, it looks the way it would through a corrective lens. No hardware, no glasses — just the image itself, adjusted before it's displayed.

December 2024 — August 2025 Android app
Done

The first version was an Android app. The correction worked inside the app — documents, videos, the built-in browser. It showed the concept was real.

It never shipped as a full product because Android's security model prevents apps from applying any overlay to other apps or the home screen. Correction was stuck inside DOA's own interface — which defeats the purpose.

September 2025 Browser extension
Discontinued

A browser extension would let the correction apply to any webpage. Development started and made progress — until it hit the same wall in a different form. Browsers block extensions from accessing the screen content they would need to apply real-time correction. Another dead end, also by design.

Both platforms protect users by restricting what software can see and touch. Those restrictions are reasonable — they just made DOA impossible on those platforms.

Where we are now
Now — Beta Windows desktop app
In progress

Windows gives DOA the OS-level access it needs to process every frame on your screen in real time — across every app, window, and content type simultaneously.

Prescription-based correction — you enter your values once, DOA applies them continuously
60fps processing — no visible lag for reading, browsing, and everyday work
Colour blindness modes — Protanopia, Deuteranopia, and Tritanopia, usable alongside correction
Multi-monitor support — different settings per screen (Premium)
Collecting feedback — real-world usage data that will improve the next stage
What's next
v1.1 Smarter starting correction
Coming soon

Right now, two people with the same prescription get the same correction. But prescriptions aren't the whole picture — screen size, viewing distance, and individual differences all affect how the correction should be applied.

Version 1.1 introduces a calibration system informed by feedback from real users across different setups. Your starting correction will be better matched to your situation from the moment you open the app — not just your numbers on paper.

Expected improvement: ~80% → ~88%

v1.2 Content-aware correction + vision change alerts
Coming soon

Reading text and watching video need different things from a correction. Text needs sharp edges and high detail. Video needs smoother correction to avoid visual noise between frames. Version 1.2 detects what's on your screen and adjusts automatically.

This version also introduces a subtle monitoring feature: if your correction preferences shift noticeably over time, DOA will suggest you consider checking in with your optometrist. Your vision can change, and DOA is well placed to notice before you do.

v2.0 Dynamic correction — your screen adapts to you
Coming soon

The biggest upgrade. Correction quality depends in part on how far you are from your screen — that distance affects how the correction should be calculated. Today, you set that distance manually once. Version 2.0 estimates it automatically in real time using your webcam, and adjusts the correction as you naturally move.

This is also the version where the correction starts feeling less like a filter and more like the display itself was made for your eyes.

Expected improvement: ~88% → ~95%

v2.5 Sharper text on every display type
Coming soon

Different monitors display pixels differently depending on their technology and layout. Version 2.5 accounts for these differences at a sub-pixel level, making text noticeably sharper on standard monitors — particularly on high-resolution displays where the difference is most visible.

Long term
macOS Mac support
Long term

The same product, built for macOS. After the Windows version is stable and the calibration system is mature.

Hardware Correction built into monitors
Long term

The furthest goal: working with display manufacturers so that correction is built directly into the monitor firmware. You sit down, it recognises your profile, and your screen adjusts automatically — no app to install, no setup. Just a display that works for your eyes.

Android — revisited Correction at the OS level on Android
Long term

The original Android idea — finally done properly. Not as an app, but as part of the operating system itself, working with device manufacturers to apply correction before anything is displayed. This is how the Android version should have always worked. It requires a different kind of partnership, which takes time — but it is on the roadmap.
